The Buck Converter Hot Loop

A synchronous buck alternates between two current loops. The inductor current is continuous, so it flows in both. The part that switches on and off abruptly is the difference between the two loops: Cin → high-side FET → low-side FET → back to Cin. That hot loop carries very fast di/dt, so its area sets the voltage spikes (V = L·di/dt) and the magnetic emissions.

Layout: the same circuit, two placements

✔ Good: C_in right at VIN/PGND pins

✘ Bad: C_in 15 mm away, loop via long traces

Consequence: with 10 nH of hot-loop inductance and 2 A switching in 5 ns, V = L·di/dt = 10 nH × 0.4 A/ns = 4 V spikes on the switch node and input rail. That means ringing, EMI failures, and possible FET overvoltage. Rule #1 of buck layout: the small input capacitor goes as close as physically possible to the VIN and PGND pins, on the same layer, with the GND plane directly beneath.
