Edges, Not Clock Rate, Set Your Bandwidth

A digital signal is a sum of sine-wave harmonics. The spectrum envelope falls at −20 dB/decade above 1/(πtpw), then at −40 dB/decade above 1/(πtr). Slow the clock and the first corner moves. Only slowing the edge moves the second one, and that corner is what drives ringing, crosstalk, and EMI.

Try this: set the clock to 1 MHz and t_r to 0.5 ns. Energy still reaches hundreds of MHz. Now raise t_r to 5 ns: the high-frequency content drops by ~20 dB. That's why a series resistor or a lower GPIO drive strength setting fixes EMI without changing the clock.
