RC Filter Explorer

A resistor and a capacitor make a first-order filter. Drag R and C and watch the corner frequency, the −20 dB/decade roll-off, the phase shift, and the step response change. The step response and the frequency response are the same filter, seen in the time domain and the frequency domain.

First principles. The capacitor's impedance 1/(2πfC) falls as frequency rises. The filter is a voltage divider between R and Z_C. At f_c the two magnitudes are equal, so |Vout/Vin| = 1/√2 (−3 dB) and the phase is −45°. One decade above f_c, Z_C is 10× smaller, so the output is 10× smaller: −20 dB/decade.
Where you meet this on a PCB: ADC anti-alias filters, the R_series × C_trace that slows every digital edge (τ = R_driver × C_load), reset-pin RC delays, and the RC "snubber" damping a switch node.
